Why do we do the things we do?

## Monetary incentive

[[Money]] is the (only) scalable & sustainable way to get collective groups of strangers to collaborate (an [[Economy]]). Money is an objective bearer of value agreeable to everyone, despite the *objects of value* being often subjective (i.e. a good appraised by individual in a trade or a market value by the collective - [[Supply and Demand]])

At an individual level, money is an ***external incentive***, bound to an economic machine[^1] and stringed to our existence - we *have* to earn money, whether we like it or not...
- Who loves being a [data labeler](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qZS50KXjAX0) sifting through the sewage of the internet all day and getting paid peanuts? Who loves putting together pieces of smart phones on an assembly line? 

> A: Jobs are often [[Alienation|alienating]] and why they suck, but we have to do them to live

### Value creation process 

The value creation process (a [[Work|job]]) has to be repeatable in itself, because...
1. Our physical needs are a constant - food, security, etc. We need them today, tomorrow, and all the days after
2. Most jobs to make such ends meet are [[Industrialization|industrialized]] in some way: mass production of goods with some standard
	- Why it's pretty tough to do creative work *as a job* that requires level of 沉淀 / distillation, like [[Art]]
		- The creative process is not repeatable / fundamentally at odds with industrialization, despite the massive demand of entertainment: you can't churn out truly creative work like an assembly line to make such ends meet[^2]
			- True creativity requires high amount of [[Agency]] in the work - experience and craftsmanship

[^2]: though we're moving towards it with LLMs

## Non-monetary incentive

Maybe I'm an idealist (and extremely privileged at that), but I've always tried to remind myself that money is a *bearer* / proxy of value. That is, while almost everything requires / made transactional with money, money itself does not fully make up for why we do things for one another. 

A provider provides service to another (say, teaching English), because, well, both the provider and the consumers see certain *intrinsic value* in that service.  

There has to be mover other than money, cultural in some way. At least, it makes me happy when thinking that way and aligning the personal values in the things I do. 

It's hollowing for me to operate entirely off of monetary incentives, if there's nothing for the money to convert to - [[Value conversion problem]]
