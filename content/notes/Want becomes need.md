I had this idea when I was in college about [[Consumerism]], that we don't really distinguish between a want and a need in modern society - what we want *is* what we need. And when something becomes a need, we consume for that need

I think this also has some root with our primal instinct for [[Hoarding]]. In the [[The Information Age|Digital age]], especially in the subculture of digital productivity - there's always new tools, new *ways* to integrate as much information and process, without deeply thinking about anything. We invent more system, more information to combat the influx of information. %%which many times collapse into themselves%%

We want so much things, without asking whether we need such things. 

I'm not saying there's no new needs that arise from a different age we live in and capabilities we have - afterall everything is always in the flux of change - [[Identity as a continuum of change|Change is itself the basis of our continuity as persons]]. What I'm saying is a genuine needs need to be *discovered*, and it's a process to carefully discern between what is a want - a manufactured need in cognition - than a genuine need rooted in reality
